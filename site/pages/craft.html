Craft & materials

From sketch to velvet

Nothing here is mass-made, and nothing here is a myth. This is the actual bench routine each piece goes through, start to finish, written down so you can hold us to it. Last updated 6 Sep 2026.

  1. 01

    Drawn by hand

    Each piece begins as a hand-drawn sketch at the SoHo atelier. Proportions are argued over on paper, not in a spreadsheet; a design lives or dies before a gram of gold moves.

  2. 02

    Cast in recycled 14k

    We work exclusively with recycled 14k gold, solid for fine pieces and vermeil (a thick layer over sterling silver) for everyday ones. Same alloy discipline, two price points, no mystery metal.

  3. 03

    Stones set by hand

    The Aurora pavé band carries 42 hand-set stones along a 2 mm comfort-fit band. Bezel settings, like the Étoile’s 0.15 ct solitaire, are pushed closed with a burnisher, one rotation at a time. That is what gives a bezel its floating look.

  4. 04

    Polished until it reads like water

    Sterling pieces are rhodium-plated for lasting brilliance, and faces like the Celestine signet’s 10×8 mm dome get a hand mirror polish. You can watch this step on a Thursday afternoon; the bench is the showroom.

  5. 05

    Engraved on request

    Signet faces and flat surfaces take hand engraving. No font library, no laser: it is cut in, which is why it survives re-polishes for decades.

  6. 06

    Packed to be lived with

    A linen box with a polishing cloth, ready to gift. And the paperwork is one page: certificate for diamonds, care card for everything, no marketing at the bottom.

Supply chain

Who makes what

Gold
Recycled 14k, sourced through a certified US refiner; solid and vermeil both trace to the same melt.
Diamonds
Conflict-free, ethically sourced, VS clarity for the solitaires; every set stone ships with its certificate.
Chains
Fourth-generation silversmiths in Arezzo, Italy, weave our rope and fine link; we finish and inspect in New York.
Pearls
Baroque freshwater, 8–9 mm, selected individually for the Twilight drops.
Plating
Rhodium over sterling, done in-house so it can be redone in-house, free, forever.
